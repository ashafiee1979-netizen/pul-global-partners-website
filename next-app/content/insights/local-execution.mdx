A mission may need staffing, language access, facilities, transport, suppliers, participant logistics, and reporting at the same time. Treating each need as a disconnected purchase can leave gaps between the handoffs.

Start with the operating environment and map the service chain: who requests, who approves, who provides, how quality is checked, and how issues are escalated. Give the client one clear view of readiness, dependencies, and daily progress while keeping specialized responsibilities with qualified partners.

## Connect the operating pieces

- Map people, vendors, language, and facilities.
- Make handoffs and escalation visible.
- Bring service evidence into one rhythm.

Local coordination is most useful when ownership is clear and information travels with the work. The right delivery model depends on the mission, country, contract, and capabilities required.
